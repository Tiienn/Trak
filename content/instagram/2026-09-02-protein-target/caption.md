You do not automatically need 1 gram of protein per pound of body weight to build muscle.

A large meta-analysis of resistance-training studies found that the added muscle-building benefit of protein appeared to level off around 1.6 g per kg of body weight per day—roughly 0.7 g per lb.

That does not make 1 g per lb “bad.” If you enjoy eating that amount or find it helps with fullness, you can keep doing it. It simply may not provide extra muscle growth compared with a lower, already-adequate intake.

The useful part? You may have more room for carbohydrates, fats, fruits and vegetables while still supporting your training.

Individual needs can change with age, health, training and whether you are dieting, so treat this as a practical reference—not a hard limit.
