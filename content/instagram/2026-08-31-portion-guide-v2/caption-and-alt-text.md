A bowl, a handful, a few cookies—these descriptions don’t always tell you how much you’re eating.

These six side-by-side examples show how changing the amount changes the calorie total. Neither portion is automatically right or wrong, and a serving on a label isn’t a personal eating target.

## Alt text

1. Same food, different portion, different calories. Three chocolate sandwich cookies compared with six. Six everyday foods, side by side. Portion awareness, not food rules.
2. Granola: a quarter US cup contains 120 kcal; half a US cup contains 240 kcal. Difference 120 kcal. Example uses Nature Valley Oats and Honey US label values.
3. Cookies: three original Oreos contain 160 kcal; six contain 320 kcal. Difference 160 kcal. US product example.
4. Cashews: 28 grams contains 160 kcal; 56 grams contains 320 kcal. Difference 160 kcal. Example uses Planters Salted Cashew Halves and Pieces US label values.
5. Cereal: 39 grams contains 140 kcal; 78 grams contains 280 kcal. Difference 140 kcal. Example uses Original Cheerios US label values, without milk.
6. Ice cream: two-thirds of a US cup contains 170 kcal; one and one-third US cups contains 340 kcal. Difference 170 kcal. Example uses Breyers Natural Vanilla US label values.
7. Bread: 50 grams contains 130 kcal; 100 grams contains 260 kcal. Difference 130 kcal. Example uses Pepperidge Farm Thin-Sliced Sourdough US label values.
8. Know the portion. Keep the foods you love. Check the amount you are eating and the label for calorie information. A label serving is not a rule for your body. Awareness, not guilt. Save this portion guide.
