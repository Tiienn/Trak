## Extracted material

The video presents two broad hypertrophy approaches: approximately 5–12 weekly sets
per muscle with very high effort, or approximately 12–20 sets while stopping 1–3
repetitions short of failure. These are examples rather than universal Trak targets.
It recommends spreading high weekly volume across sessions, teaching beginners a small
set of movement patterns, and individualising exercise selection as training experience
increases. The video supplies no general repetition range, rest interval, starting-load
rule, progression method, or cardio target.

## Approved catalogue work

Trak adds converging machine chest press, lat pulldown, chest-supported machine row,
hack squat, Bayesian cable curl, and dumbbell skull crusher. Prescriptions are
conservative Trak defaults rather than directions copied from the video:

| Exercise | Primary / secondary | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Converging machine chest press | Chest / arms, shoulders | 3 × 6–12; 120–180s; 1–3 RIR |
| Lat pulldown | Back / arms, shoulders | 3 × 8–15; 90–180s; 1–3 RIR |
| Chest-supported machine row | Back / shoulders, arms | 3 × 8–15; 90–180s; 1–3 RIR |
| Hack squat | Legs / glutes, abs | 3 × 8–15; 120–180s; 1–3 RIR |
| Bayesian cable curl | Arms / forearms | 2 × 8–15; 60–120s; 1–3 RIR |
| Dumbbell skull crusher | Arms / shoulders | 2 × 8–15; 60–120s; 1–3 RIR |

All use double progression: add controlled repetitions to the top of the range, then
make the smallest practical load increase. Cross-body cable lateral raise is retained
as a variation of the existing lateral raise. Machine hip thrust, Romanian deadlift,
and standing calf raise remain existing entries and receive this source reference.

## Approved recommendation behavior

- Comfortable exercises receive a modest ranking preference only while goal, equipment,
  location, time, recovery, and limitation checks still pass.
- An uncomfortable response lowers priority. An unsuitable response removes the exercise
  from recommendations until the user changes the response.
- Completion and tracked progressive-overload history remain positive signals.
- A generated session cannot exceed ten recommended direct sets for one muscle. This is
  a forward-looking guardrail, not a target to reach.
- Existing rotation groups include the new alternatives.
- The existing safe failure policy remains: beginners keep 1–2 clean reps in reserve;
  experienced users may test technical failure only on the final set of selected stable
  machine or isolation movements. Loaded compounds and the dumbbell skull crusher are
  not prescribed to failure.
- Weekly Muscle Score remains one point per completed primary-muscle working set, capped
  at 100% at 12 sets. Recommendations and feedback earn no points.

## Evidence checks

- Training at 1–2 RIR produced similar quadriceps hypertrophy to momentary failure with
  less repetition loss and acute fatigue in an eight-week within-participant trial:
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38393985/
- Equal-volume resistance training performed two or four times weekly produced similar
  growth and strength changes, so the video's “up to 30% faster” frequency claim is not
  encoded: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35069251/
- Auto-regulated exercise selection produced small advantages in one trial of trained
  men, but this does not establish pump or soreness as reliable selection tests:
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29016481/
- Early muscle damage and soreness are not treated as evidence of subsequent hypertrophy:
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27219125/
- A recent crossover trial did not find greater resistance-training volume after a
  high-carbohydrate pre-exercise meal than after lower-carbohydrate or placebo meals:
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40029149/

## Not adopted

- A promise or expected timeline for gaining 20 lb of muscle.
- “Growth nearly doubles” or “30% faster” as product claims.
- Universal 5–12 or 12–20-set prescriptions.
- Every working set taken to absolute failure.
- Pump or next-day soreness as proof that an exercise is effective.
- Automatic calorie changes based on estimated 20% male or 30% female body-fat cutoffs.
- Fixed pre-workout carbohydrate timing, creatine prompts, or supplement recommendations.
- Cardio changes; the source provides no cardio prescription.

## Product decision

The useful material becomes exercise variety, safer rotation, a per-session set guardrail,
and explicit user feedback. The creator's routine, marketing outcomes, and nutrition or
supplement claims are not copied. Only completed training updates Workout Time and Weekly
Muscle Score.
