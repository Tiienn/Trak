## Experiment and coaching material

The video follows three people for 30 days. One side was trained daily and the other
side twice weekly. The daily side received roughly 35 hard sets per week versus about
10 on the comparison side. Arms, legs, and chest were each represented by a different
participant. The creator reports larger MRI changes and a strength advantage on each
daily-trained side, but acknowledges that this is only a three-person experiment.

- 0:00–1:31: protocol and daily volume.
- 1:32–2:12: recovery and muscle-protein-synthesis discussion.
- 2:13–4:59: soreness, fatigue, and the repeated-bout effect.
- 4:10: Bulgarian split squat example.
- 5:00–6:03: strength changes.
- 6:04–7:28: sharp knee pain, load reduction, exercise rotation, and tendon concerns.
- 8:45–11:59: MRI results and limitations.
- 12:06–12:44: practical specialization advice.
- 12:55–13:05: exercise rotation-pair graphic.

The spoken leg protocol says five sets of 9–12 repetitions, while the supplied video
description said four sets. This discrepancy is retained here and neither value is
encoded as a Trak prescription. The video provides no cardio prescription.

## Approved rotation relationships

- Chest: flat dumbbell press / machine chest fly.
- Shoulders: dumbbell shoulder press / lateral raise.
- Back: pull-up / cable row.
- Biceps: cable curl / preacher curl.
- Triceps: rope triceps pushdown / overhead cable triceps extension.
- Glutes: hip thrust / weighted step-up.
- Quads: leg extension / leg press.

Recent completion of one movement modestly favours its paired alternative when both
fit the user’s goal, experience, equipment, location, time, and limitations.

## Approved specialization guardrail

Specialization is an explicit option, never inferred from the video or automatically
enabled. It is available only to intermediate and advanced users. A block must provide
the priority muscle, baseline weekly sets, and current week. Trak:

- accepts only weeks 1–6;
- permits a target increase of 2–4 direct sets per week;
- never recommends a specialization dose when fewer than two sets remain under the cap;
- recommends at most three priority sets in one item;
- rotates paired movements when recent history is available;
- continues to enforce equipment, location, time, experience, and limitation filters.

Displaying or accepting a recommendation earns no points. Only marking the training
complete records Workout Time and completed sets for Weekly Muscle Score.

## Rejected material

- Daily resistance training as a default recommendation.
- A universal target of 35 hard sets per muscle per week.
- “Almost four times faster” as a product or scientific claim.
- Linear extrapolation of a 30-day percentage to annual muscle growth.
- The claim that muscle growth reliably halves every training year.
- Soreness as the sole readiness or recovery measure.
- Treating these three MRI outcomes as expected user results.
- The claim that more than half of the daily-bench study participants were injured;
  this could not be verified from the accessible primary record.
