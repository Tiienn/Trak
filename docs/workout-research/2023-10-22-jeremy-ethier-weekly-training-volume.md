# Workout research: “How Many Sets Do You Really Need to Build Muscle?”

Status: approved for Weekly Muscle Score guidance on 2026-09-02, with fractional
secondary-muscle credit explicitly rejected.

## Internal source

- URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PQWw0BJc5LQ

- Creator: Jeremy Ethier
- Published: 2023-10-22

- Purpose: internal workout research only; never a user-facing YouTube feature.

## Extracted programming guidance

This video contains no exercises or cardio prescription to add to the catalogue. It
discusses resistance-training volume for hypertrophy across beginner through advanced
experience levels. Equipment, load, and repetition ranges are not prescribed.

- 0:25: a 2017 analysis grouped weekly volume into 1–5, 5–9, and 10-or-more
  sets per muscle and reported a general dose-response relationship.
- 2:06: 10–20 weekly sets is presented as a broad population-level range rather
  than an individual requirement.
- 2:21: individual hypertrophy and recovery responses can vary.
- 4:57: useful working sets require meaningful effort and controlled technique.
- 5:07: longer interset rest may preserve performance and make lower volume more
  productive.
- 5:54: direct and indirect muscle work should not automatically be treated as
  equivalent.
- 8:01: lower volumes can still stimulate growth; sub-target work is not worthless.
- 9:08: beginners should start toward the lower end rather than chase maximum volume.
- 10:00: add about two weekly sets only after checking progress, sleep, nutrition,
  technique, and recovery.
- 10:08: reduce volume or take a break when recovery is inadequate.

## Approved Weekly Muscle Score behavior

- Keep 12 completed working sets for a named muscle as the 100% weekly target.
- Award exactly one point for every counted working set. No fractional `0.5` credit.
- Count catalogue completions only for their explicitly recorded primary muscles;
  do not infer or auto-credit secondary muscles.
- Treat warm-up sets as non-scoring. The logger describes entered sets as working sets.
- Keep the score capped at 100%; additional volume does not earn bonus points.
- Explain that 100% means “weekly target met,” not maximum possible muscle growth.
- Show coaching bands: 1–5 some stimulus, 6–11 building toward target, 12–15 target
  met, 16–19 check recovery, and 20-or-more high volume.
- Exclude the catch-all `Other` category from scored targets while retaining it for
  workout logging.
- Do not award extra points for failure, forced repetitions, drop sets, rest duration,
  or training frequency.
- Recommendations consider trailing-seven-day completed sets, omit target-met muscles
  from attention areas, and progressively deprioritize a muscle at 16 and 20 sets.
- Recommendations themselves never earn points. Only completed training updates
  Workout Time and Weekly Muscle Score.

## Evidence checks and limitations

- Schoenfeld et al. (2017) found a graded volume relationship, but the three-level
  categorical comparison was only a statistical trend. The video's claim that 5–9
  sets produce “about 80%” of maximum growth is not used as a score formula:
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27433992/
- Baz-Valle et al. found 12–20 sets potentially useful for quadriceps and biceps in
  young trained men, with important population and set-counting limitations:
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8884877/
- Pelland et al. found diminishing returns and modelled indirect work separately.
  Although fractional counting fit their population-level model best, Trak deliberately
  retains simpler full-point, explicitly selected muscle accounting:
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41343037/
- A trial in trained young men found better adaptations with three-minute than
  one-minute rests, but this does not justify changing points based on rest duration:
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26605807/
- The video's individual-response example is internally inconsistent: narration calls
  it a 2019 study while the description says 2016. Its exact “19 participants, one-third
  each” account could not be reconciled confidently with the matching published trials,
  so it is not used as a scoring rule.

## Rejected material

- Fractional or automatic secondary-muscle points.
- Treating 10–20 sets as a universal prescription or medical threshold.
- Converting the “about 80%” claim into a score curve.
- Rewarding all-out failure, forced reps, rest-pause work, or drop sets with extra points.
- Assuming that more than 12 sets deserves a score above 100%.
- Adding the creator's downloadable routines or copying a routine blindly.

## Product decision

Weekly Muscle Score remains a transparent adherence and muscle-coverage indicator, not
a prediction of hypertrophy. Seven named muscles receive one point per completed working
set and reach 100% at 12 sets. Higher volume changes coaching and recommendation priority,
not points. `Other` remains available for logging without a universal weekly target.
