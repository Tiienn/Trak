## Approved material

- Overhead cable and dumbbell triceps extensions at 6:51.
- Incline dumbbell press at 7:15.
- Machine chest fly at 7:25, without adopting the unsupported “forward lean targets upper chest” claim.
- Lateral raise at 9:29.
- Pull-up and an assisted progression at 9:31.
- Moderate steady cardio and carefully gated vigorous intervals at 2:40–3:13.
- Baseline-relative step progression as coaching context, not a fixed 10,000-step rule.
- A conservative rate-of-loss idea may inform future guardrails for lean, resistance-trained users, but 0.7% per week is not encoded as a universal maximum.

Strength prescriptions in the catalogue are Trak defaults, not prescriptions stated in the video. The video supplied no complete routine and no strength sets, reps, rest, or loading plan.

## Rejected material

- “Stubbornness” scores and fixed body-fat thresholds.
- Diagnosing visceral fat from appearance or abdominal firmness.
- Sex or ethnicity rules inferred from private DXA examples.
- Body weight in pounds multiplied by 10–13 as a calorie target.
- Universal under-2,000-calorie or 10,000-step instructions.
- Promises about face puffiness resolving within 24 hours.
- Any claim that an exercise burns fat from a named body area.
- Calorie-burn targets for cardio recommendations.

## Approved weekly-cardio expansion

The source video supports the following coaching ideas:

- 2:16–2:53 — combine resistance training and aerobic exercise rather than treating cardio as a replacement for strength.
- 2:55–2:59 — 15–30 minutes after strength is one practical placement, not a mandatory rule for every workout.
- 2:59–3:07 — a harder cardio session may be used once or twice weekly.
- 3:07–3:13 — do not begin with an all-out effort.
- 3:15–3:29 — cardio supports the process, while an energy deficit remains the primary weight-loss driver.
- 7:57–8:10 — daily walking can increase activity, but Trak does not impose a universal 8,000–10,000-step target.
- 12:00–12:31 — additional movement can be considered as progress slows, without automatically escalating from a single weight measurement.

Trak adapts that material using broader evidence rather than copying the video's routine:

- The lose-weight goal uses a 150 moderate-equivalent-minute rolling seven-day baseline, with visible 60, 90, and 120 minute milestones. Progress beyond 150 toward 300 is optional rather than automatically prescribed.
- One completed moderate minute counts as one equivalent minute. One completed vigorous minute counts as two. Light cardio is tracked separately and does not inflate the baseline.
- Legacy cardio logs without an intensity are interpreted conservatively as moderate.
- A user with no recent qualifying cardio starts with 15 moderate minutes. Later recommendations can use up to the catalogue session maximum while respecting available time and the number of equivalent minutes remaining.
- Trak does not force another cardio recommendation once the 150-minute baseline is met.
- Vigorous cardio remains limited to intermediate or advanced users, is withheld after two vigorous sessions in the rolling window, and is withheld immediately after substantial leg/glute training. Existing recorded health and joint limitations still apply.
- Completed cardio intensity is persisted for recommended workouts, manual workout logs, and custom training-plan items.
- Cardio recommendations and Weekly Cardio progress do not award points. Workout Time and cardio progress change only after completion; strength sets alone update Weekly Muscle Score.

Additional evidence for this expansion:

- U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, *Physical Activity Guidelines for Americans, 2nd edition*: 150–300 moderate minutes, 75–150 vigorous minutes, or an equivalent combination, plus muscle strengthening on at least two days. https://odphp.health.gov/sites/default/files/2019-09/Physical_Activity_Guidelines_2nd_edition.pdf
- Jayedi et al., *Aerobic Exercise and Weight Loss in Adults: A Systematic Review and Dose-Response Meta-Analysis*: benefits increased through 300 weekly minutes, with approximately 150 moderate-to-vigorous minutes associated with clinically important waist and body-fat reductions. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39724371/
- Wewege et al., *The effects of high-intensity interval training vs. moderate-intensity continuous training on body composition in overweight and obese adults*: both approaches produced modest improvements, without a significant body-composition advantage for HIIT. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28401638/
- Binmahfoz et al., *Effect of resistance exercise on body composition, muscle strength and cardiometabolic health during dietary weight loss*: resistance exercise protected fat-free mass and improved fat loss and strength compared with diet-only weight loss. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40909191/

The video's 15–30 minute suggestion, one-to-two hard sessions, and combined strength/cardio principle are source-derived. The 150-minute baseline, intensity-equivalent accounting, milestones, legacy-data behavior, and recovery gates are evidence-informed Trak adaptations.
